Tech roster — Each tech's name, skill level (junior/mid/senior), certifications, specialties (water heaters, gas lines, drain clearing, remodels), current location, current job status (available, en route, on-site, wrapping up), and historical performance data (average job time by type, callback rate, customer rating).

Customer database — Name, address, phone, customer since date, lifetime value, job history, notes (preferences like "always use the side door," "has aggressive dog," "repeat referral source"), communication preferences (call vs. text), and payment history (pays on time, slow payer, has disputed before).

Schedule — Today's jobs with time slots, assigned tech, job type, estimated duration, customer linked, status (scheduled, in progress, completed, rescheduled). Plus the next 5-7 days for rebooking displaced appointments.

Job catalog — Every service Shamrock offers with pricing ranges, estimated duration, required skill level, required parts, and urgency classification rules (burst pipe = emergency, dripping faucet = routine, no hot water = same-day).

Service area map — Zip codes or boundaries Blake serves, with drive time estimates between common areas. Doesn't need to be real GPS, just enough to make dispatch decisions ("Jake is in Lehi, the emergency is in Saratoga Springs, that's 20 minutes").

Parts inventory — What's on each truck, what's in the shop, common parts needed by job type, supplier info for rush orders.

Communication templates — How Blake talks to customers. Confirmation texts, reschedule messages, follow-up messages, emergency dispatch notifications. These need to sound like Blake, not like a corporation.

Intent statements — Blake's business rules from the interview. These aren't data exactly, they're the judgment layer that sits on top of everything else.

Business rules and policies — Warranty terms, callback policy, after-hours pricing multiplier, cancellation policy, payment terms.

Historical patterns — Seasonal trends (frozen pipes in December, AC-related plumbing in July), average jobs per day, typical no-show rate, busiest days of the week.
