Here's the full emergency triage flow broken into 6 phases. Let me walk through what the AI is actually deciding at each step:

Phase 1 — Intake: The AI doesn't just log the call. It actively assesses severity by asking targeted questions (active water flow? gas smell? safety risk?) and classifies it as a true emergency vs. urgent-but-not-emergency. Non-emergencies get fast-tracked to the next available slot instead of triggering the full cascade.

Phase 2 — Find tech: This is where the AI's knowledge of Blake's team matters. It evaluates every tech by skill match (don't send the apprentice to a gas leak), proximity, and what they're currently doing. If nobody is free, it ranks current jobs by "bumpability" — non-urgent jobs for new customers get bumped before a repeat customer's scheduled repair.

Phase 3 — Dispatch: The AI checks if the tech can safely stop their current job before pulling them. It notifies the emergency customer with a specific ETA, tech name, and pricing (after-hours rate if applicable).

Phase 4 — Cascade: This is where intent engineering shines. Every displaced customer gets a different notification based on Blake's business values. A 5-year repeat customer gets a personal apology and priority rebook. A brand new customer gets a professional reschedule with a courtesy offer.

Phase 5 — Recover: The AI rebuilds the rest of the day's schedule around the disruption, reoptimizing routes and timing across all techs. Then it sends Blake a brief: here's what happened, here's what I decided, here's why.

Phase 6 — Learn: The system logs the entire event so next time a similar emergency hits, it's smarter about which techs to pull, how long emergencies typically take, and which customers handled rescheduling well vs. which ones churned.
